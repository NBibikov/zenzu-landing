## The Short Answer

When your flashcard reviews pile up, don't try to clear them all in one sitting. Triage first: cut new cards to zero, do a fixed number of overdue reviews per day (50-80 is sustainable for most learners), and aggressively delete or suspend cards you no longer need. A backlog of 500 cards isn't a crisis — it's a signal to adjust your pace, not your discipline.

## Why the Pile-Up Happens

Spaced repetition systems schedule reviews based on when your memory is predicted to fade — not when you have free time. Miss a few busy days and the system keeps scheduling new reviews on top of old ones. This is normal. Piotr Woźniak, who pioneered algorithmic spaced repetition with SuperMemo in the late 1980s, documented this exact failure mode decades ago: backlogs form whenever life interrupts a review schedule, and panic-cramming them makes retention worse, not better.

## Step 1: A Late Card Isn't a Lost Card

Here's the reassuring math. If a card had a 30-day interval and you're reviewing it 5 days late, you've only added about 17% extra decay time — your memory strength hasn't collapsed. Modern scheduling algorithms like FSRS-6 (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler, built on large-scale review data published in 2023-2024) model this decay curve directly, so a slightly overdue card still gets scheduled sensibly once you review it. You're not starting over; you're just continuing a slightly stretched curve.

## Step 2: Freeze New Cards Immediately

The single biggest mistake is adding new vocabulary while old reviews pile up. Set new-card intake to zero until your backlog is under control. Every new card you add today is a review you'll owe in 1, 3, 7, and 21 days — compounding the exact problem you're trying to fix.

## Step 3: Set a Daily Cap, Not a Deadline

Don't try to zero out 400 overdue cards in one day. Research on the spacing effect — most notably Cepeda et al.'s 2006 meta-analysis in *Psychological Bulletin* covering 254 studies — shows that cramming large volumes of review in a single session produces weaker long-term retention than the same volume spread across several days, even when total review time is identical.

Practical plan:
- **Day 1-3:** Review 60-80 cards per day, oldest-overdue first.
- **Day 4-7:** Continue at the same pace; the backlog shrinks because fewer new reviews are being generated.
- **Week 2:** Once overdue count drops below your normal daily volume, resume adding 5-10 new cards per day.

## Step 4: Prune Before You Review

Before diving in, scan the backlog for cards that no longer serve you:

- Words you now know cold and never confuse — delete.
- Duplicates or near-duplicates — merge or delete.
- Words from a context you've abandoned (a show you stopped watching, a trip that happened) — suspend.

Cutting 20-30% of a bloated backlog this way is common, and it's not cheating — it's curation. A smaller, relevant deck reviewed consistently beats a sprawling one reviewed sporadically.

## Step 5: Shorten the Session, Not the Commitment

If 80 cards feels overwhelming in one block, split it into two 10-minute sessions. Robert Bjork's research on "desirable difficulty" (Bjork & Bjork, 2011) suggests that short, slightly effortful retrieval sessions spaced through the day strengthen memory more than one long grinding block — which also makes backlog clearing feel less punishing.

## Preventing the Next Pile-Up

- Cap new cards per day (5-10 is realistic for sustained study).
- Review at the same time daily so it becomes automatic rather than optional.
- When you know a busy stretch is coming, pause new cards preemptively instead of reactively.

## FAQ

**Will skipped reviews ruin my memory of a word?**
No — forgetting is gradual, not a cliff. A card reviewed a few days late is still far better retained than one never reviewed again.

**Should I just reset the whole deck?**
Rarely. Resetting throws away real memory strength. Prune and reschedule instead.

**How many overdue cards is "too many"?**
If your backlog exceeds roughly 10x your normal daily review count, freeze new cards and start the staged catch-up above.

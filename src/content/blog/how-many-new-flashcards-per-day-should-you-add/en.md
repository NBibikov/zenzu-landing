## The Short Answer

Most learners should add **5 to 20 new flashcards per day**, depending on how much daily review time they can sustain. Beginners (A1-A2) do best around 10-15 new cards; intermediate and advanced learners (B1+) can push to 15-25 because they already have a web of known vocabulary to hang new words on. The real limit isn't motivation — it's your review queue. Add more new cards than your schedule can digest, and within two weeks you'll face a backlog so large you quit reviewing altogether.

## Why There's a Real Ceiling

Every flashcard you learn today doesn't disappear — it comes back for review tomorrow, in a few days, then in a week, then in a month. This is the backbone of **spaced repetition**, first described mathematically by Hermann Ebbinghaus in 1885 and later turned into software by Piotr Wozniak with SuperMemo in the late 1980s. Cepeda et al. (2006), reviewing over 300 studies, confirmed that spacing reviews over time produces dramatically better long-term retention than cramming.

The catch: each new card you add today creates roughly 5-9 future reviews over the next two months as the algorithm spaces out repetitions. If you add 30 new cards a day for a month, you're not reviewing 30 cards — you're reviewing hundreds, compounding daily.

## The Math Behind a Sustainable Number

A rough rule used by long-time Anki and SuperMemo users: **budget about 1-1.5 minutes per new card's total lifetime review load**, spread across weeks. In practice:

- **10 new cards/day** → roughly 15-25 minutes of total daily study (new + reviews) once your deck matures
- **20 new cards/day** → roughly 30-45 minutes daily at maturity
- **30+ new cards/day** → 60+ minutes daily, which is where most self-learners burn out

If you only have 15-20 minutes a day, cap new cards at 5-10. If you can commit 30-40 minutes, 15-20 is sustainable. Don't set your new-card limit based on enthusiasm on day one — set it based on the time you can realistically protect every single day for the next six months.

## Adjust by Level, Not Just by Time

### Beginners (A1-A2)
New vocabulary has no existing hooks in your brain yet — every word is unfamiliar sounds attached to unfamiliar meaning. Keep it to **8-12 cards/day** and prioritize high-frequency words (the top 1,000 words cover roughly 80% of everyday conversation).

### Intermediate (B1-B2)
You already recognize patterns, cognates, and grammar structures. **15-20 cards/day** is manageable because new words attach to things you already know.

### Advanced (C1+)
At this stage you're mostly adding nuance — idioms, collocations, rare words. Volume matters less than context. **10-15 cards/day**, each tied to a rich example sentence, beats mindless volume.

## Signs You're Adding Too Many

- Your daily review count keeps climbing week over week instead of leveling off
- You start hitting "again" (forgot) on more than 30% of reviews
- You dread opening the app because the queue feels endless
- You start cramming reviews in one sitting instead of little and often

If you see any of these, cut your new-card limit in half for two weeks. The backlog will shrink, your accuracy will climb back up, and reviewing will feel manageable again.

## A Simple Weekly Check

Once a week, look at your average daily review count. If it's growing faster than you can comfortably handle, lower your new-card cap by 20-30%. If reviews feel easy and quick, you can raise it by a few cards. This isn't a one-time decision — it's a dial you adjust based on real data from your own study sessions.

## FAQ

**Is there one universal number that works for everyone?**
No. Available daily time, language difficulty, and your existing vocabulary all change the right number. Start at 10 and adjust from there.

**Does adding more cards speed up fluency?**
Only up to the point your review queue stays sustainable. Beyond that, forgotten cards pile up and actually slow you down.

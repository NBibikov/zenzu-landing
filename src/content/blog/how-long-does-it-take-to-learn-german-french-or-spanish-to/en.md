Reaching B2 takes roughly **600–700 hours of focused study** in French or Spanish, and **750–900 hours** in German. At one hour a day, that's about 20 months for French/Spanish and 24–30 months for German — but the real number depends heavily on how you study, not just how long.

## The hour benchmarks, by source

These figures come from two of the most-cited frameworks in language teaching:

- **FSI (US Foreign Service Institute)** groups French and Spanish as "Category I" languages, needing around 600 class hours to reach general professional fluency (roughly C1). B2 sits at about 70–80% of that path: **500–600 hours**.
- German is "Category II," requiring closer to **900 hours** for the same proficiency, because of case declensions, verb-position rules, and compound-noun vocabulary that slow down early reading comprehension.
- **Goethe-Institut's** own course-hour guidelines for German list cumulative totals of roughly 80–200 hours for A1, 350–400 for B1, and **650–800 for B2**.
- **Instituto Cervantes** and the **Alliance Française** publish similar cumulative ranges for Spanish and French, usually landing at **500–650 hours** for B2.

These are classroom-hour estimates. Self-directed learners without a teacher or immersion environment often need 20–30% more, since you lose some efficiency without live correction — but you can claw much of that back with the right tools (more below).

## How intensity changes your timeline

The hour total is fixed; your pace is not.

| Study rhythm | Hours/week | French/Spanish B2 | German B2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual (20 min/day) | ~2.5 | ~4.5 years | ~6 years |
| Steady (1 hr/day) | ~7 | ~19 months | ~24 months |
| Serious (2 hrs/day) | ~14 | ~10 months | ~13 months |
| Intensive (4+ hrs/day, immersion) | ~28 | ~5 months | ~6.5 months |

This is why "6 months to fluency" claims are usually about A2/B1 conversational comfort, not true B2 (CEFR's own descriptor: independently discussing abstract topics, understanding native-speed media, writing structured arguments).

## Why German lags behind French and Spanish

Spanish and French share Latin roots, cognates, and relatively regular phonics with English speakers' existing vocabulary — Krashen's input hypothesis (1982) suggests comprehensible input becomes available faster when structural overlap with your native language is higher. German's four cases, separable verbs, and gendered nouns mean learners spend more hours just stabilizing grammar before input becomes "comprehensible" at native speed.

## The three levers that actually shorten your timeline

1. **Comprehensible input volume.** Hours spent reading and listening to material you mostly understand (90%+) compound faster than grammar drills alone.
2. **Spaced repetition.** Hermann Ebbinghaus mapped the forgetting curve in 1885: without review, you lose most new vocabulary within days. Algorithmic spacing (modern systems use FSRS-5) cuts review time by reviewing words right before you'd forget them, not on a fixed daily schedule.
3. **Output under light pressure.** Writing short summaries or speaking aloud daily, even for 5 minutes, forces retrieval — the single strongest predictor of retention in vocabulary-acquisition research.

## A realistic 12-month plan toward B2

- **Months 1–3 (A1→A2):** 45–60 min/day. Core grammar + 1,000–1,500 high-frequency words via graded reading.
- **Months 4–7 (A2→B1):** 60–75 min/day. Shift to native podcasts, short articles, and daily 10-minute speaking practice.
- **Months 8–12 (B1→B2):** 75–90 min/day. Native YouTube content, news articles, and writing 150-word responses twice a week.

Total: roughly 450–550 hours for French/Spanish (close to B2), or extend to 15 months for German's extra 200 hours.

## FAQ

**Can I reach B2 faster than these estimates?** Yes, with daily immersion (living abroad, intensive courses) — but the hour count rarely drops below 400–500 even then.

**Does B1 "conversational fluency" count as B2?** No. B1 is functional; B2 means arguing a point, understanding fast native speech, and reading unsimplified text comfortably.

**Is German really 50% harder?** Not harder to understand long-term, just slower to unlock input in the first 200–300 hours.

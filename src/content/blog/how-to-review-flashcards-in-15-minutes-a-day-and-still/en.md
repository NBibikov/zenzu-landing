You can absolutely make real progress in a language with just 15 minutes of flashcard review a day — if you use spaced repetition instead of cramming, keep sessions to 15-25 cards, and review at the same time daily so the habit sticks. The key isn't more time; it's reviewing the *right* cards at the *right* moment, which is exactly what spaced repetition algorithms are built to calculate for you.

## Why 15 Minutes Is Enough (If You Do It Right)

Hermann Ebbinghaus mapped the "forgetting curve" back in 1885: without review, we lose roughly 50-80% of new information within a day or two. But his research also showed the fix — reviewing material right before you'd forget it resets the curve and makes the memory stronger each time.

This is the basis of spaced repetition. A 2006 meta-analysis by Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, and Rohrer (reviewing over 300 studies) found that spacing reviews across days produces dramatically better long-term retention than reviewing the same amount of material in one sitting. You don't need more repetitions — you need them spread out correctly.

Modern schedulers like FSRS-6 (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) take this further than older fixed-interval systems. Instead of a rigid "review every 3 days," FSRS models each card's individual difficulty and your personal forgetting curve, scheduling reviews at the exact point you're about to forget. That precision is what makes 15 minutes productive instead of wasted.

## The 15-Minute Routine, Minute by Minute

**Minutes 0-2: Set up and warm up.** Open your deck, glance at how many cards are due. Don't read ahead — just start.

**Minutes 2-10: Active recall, not recognition.** For each card, say or write the answer *before* flipping it. This is the "testing effect," documented by Roediger and Karpicke (2006): actively retrieving an answer strengthens memory far more than passively re-reading it. Rate each card honestly (easy, good, hard, forgot) — the algorithm only works if you're truthful.

**Minutes 10-13: Focus on the hard cards.** Most schedulers resurface difficult cards sooner within the same session. Spend extra seconds building a mental image or a sentence around the word — don't just reread the translation.

**Minutes 13-15: Add 3-5 new cards.** Keep new material small and sustainable. Adding 50 new words in one sitting guarantees an overwhelming backlog within a week.

## A Worked Example

Say you're learning German and today's due card is the word *verpassen* (to miss, as in an opportunity or train).

1. You see the front: *verpassen* — you try to recall the meaning before flipping.
2. You check: "to miss."
3. You rate it "hard" because you confused it with *vermissen* (to miss someone emotionally).
4. The algorithm notes the difficulty and schedules the next review sooner — maybe in 2 days instead of 6 — and might also resurface *vermissen* soon so you can compare the two directly.
5. Next time, instead of re-reading the definition, you recall the source sentence: *"Ich habe den Zug verpasst"* (I missed the train). Context does the heavy lifting your brain needs.

This loop — recall, rate, reschedule — is the entire engine of efficient review.

## Common Mistakes That Waste the 15 Minutes

- **Marking everything "easy" to feel good.** This breaks the scheduling and cards resurface too late, after you've already forgotten them.
- **Cramming all new cards at once.** Piotr Woźniak's decades of SuperMemo research consistently show that overloading new material causes review backlogs that collapse habits entirely.
- **Reviewing at random times.** Tying the session to an existing habit (coffee, commute, lunch) matters more than the specific time of day.
- **Skipping days "to catch up later."** Spacing only works if intervals stay roughly on schedule — a 3-day gap that becomes 10 days defeats the algorithm's timing.

## FAQ

**Is 15 minutes really enough to learn a language?**
For vocabulary retention specifically, yes — it's enough to maintain and grow a review deck of hundreds of words over months. Pair it with listening or reading for full fluency.

**What if I have more than 25 cards due?**
Finish the session at 15 minutes and let the backlog clear over the next few days rather than burning out in one long sitting.

**Should I review on weekends too?**
Yes — consistency beats intensity. Daily 15-minute sessions outperform a single 2-hour weekend cram, per the spacing research above.

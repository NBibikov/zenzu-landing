## What Is Sentence Mining?

Sentence mining is the practice of extracting unknown words and phrases from authentic content you're already reading, watching, or listening to — then turning each one into a flashcard that includes the full sentence it came from. Instead of memorizing isolated vocabulary lists ("maison = house"), you learn words the way they actually appear in speech and writing, complete with grammar, collocations, and tone. It's the single fastest way to build vocabulary because every card is drawn from material you care about and understand almost completely already.

## Why Sentences Beat Word Lists

Paul Nation's research on vocabulary acquisition (Nation, 2001) found that words learned in meaningful context are retained far longer than words memorized as translation pairs, because context gives your brain multiple retrieval cues — meaning, grammar position, and sound. This matches Stephen Krashen's input hypothesis: comprehension happens best when new material is just slightly above your current level (often called "i+1").

Sentence mining operationalizes this idea. You don't study vocabulary in a vacuum; you study the exact sentence where a word blocked your comprehension, so the card teaches you grammar and usage for free.

### The Spaced Repetition Connection

Mined sentences are only useful if you actually review them before forgetting. This is where spaced repetition matters. Modern scheduling algorithms like FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler), which outperformed older algorithms like SM-2 in independent benchmarking in 2023, predict exactly when you're about to forget a specific card and resurface it right before that point — far more efficiently than fixed intervals.

## The 4-Step Sentence Mining Routine

**1. Pick input at the right difficulty.** Aim for content where you understand roughly 90–98% of words — a sweet spot supported by Nation's research on unknown-word density and comprehension. A news article, podcast transcript, or YouTube video with subtitles works well.

**2. Mine, don't translate everything.** Read or listen actively. When you hit a word or phrase you don't know, mark the whole sentence, not just the word.

**3. Build a context-rich flashcard.** Each card should contain: the target word, the full source sentence, a translation, and audio pronunciation. Example in Spanish:

- Sentence: *"A pesar de la lluvia, decidieron salir a caminar."*
- Target: *a pesar de* (despite)
- Translation: "Despite the rain, they decided to go for a walk."

**4. Review on a spaced schedule.** Study new cards daily, but let an algorithm — not your gut — decide when old ones resurface.

## Worked Example: German

Say you're watching a German interview and hear: *"Er hat sich endlich dazu entschlossen, umzuziehen."* You don't know *entschlossen* (decided, resolved). Instead of a flat word-pair card, mine the full sentence:

- Front: *"Er hat sich endlich dazu ___, umzuziehen."*
- Back: *entschlossen* — "He finally decided to move."

Because you already understood the rest of the sentence, your brain locks in *entschlossen* alongside the reflexive construction *sich entschließen zu*, getting grammar and vocabulary in one card.

## How Many Sentences Per Day?

Most learners can sustainably add 10–20 new sentence cards per day. Going higher creates review backlogs that cause burnout within a few weeks — a pattern well documented among heavy Anki users. Quality of context matters more than volume: five well-chosen sentences from content you enjoy beat twenty mined from a textbook you're bored by.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mining single words without sentences.** You lose grammar and collocation context, the whole point of the method.
- **Mining too much at once.** Overloaded review queues are the #1 reason people quit spaced repetition systems.
- **Skipping audio.** Pronunciation anchors a word in memory far better than text alone.
- **Choosing content that's too hard.** If you understand less than 90% of a passage, mining becomes translation work, not learning.

## FAQ

**Is sentence mining better than vocabulary apps with pre-made decks?**
Pre-made decks are faster to start but less personalized. Sentence mining takes slightly longer to set up per word but produces stronger retention because the context is meaningful to you.

**How long until I see results?**
Most learners notice smoother reading and listening comprehension after 4–6 weeks of mining 10–15 sentences daily, once a few hundred cards have entered review rotation.

**Can I mine from videos and podcasts, not just text?**
Yes — audio and video sources are ideal because they add natural pronunciation and intonation to each card.

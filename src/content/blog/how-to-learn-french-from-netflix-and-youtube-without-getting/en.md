## How to Learn French from Netflix and YouTube Without Getting Lost

You learn French from Netflix and YouTube by matching subtitle mode to your actual level, capping passive watching to a quarter of your study time, and converting only the words you'll meet again into flashcards reviewed on a spaced schedule. Video alone doesn't build French — it builds vague familiarity. The gap between "I watched 40 hours of *Lupin*" and "I can hold a conversation" is almost always a missing extraction and review step.

### Why most video-based learners plateau

Comprehensible input matters — Stephen Krashen's input hypothesis (1982) is the reason video feels productive. But Krashen's model assumes input slightly above your current level ("i+1"), not native-speed dialogue with idioms, slang, and cultural references stacked on top of each other. Dropping a B1 learner into *Dix Pour Cent* with no subtitles isn't input, it's noise. Noise doesn't get encoded; it gets tuned out after 20 minutes, which is why so many people "watch French for months" and plateau.

### Match subtitle mode to your level, not your pride

- **A1–A2**: English subtitles first pass, French subtitles second pass on the same clip (5-10 minutes max). You're building ear-to-word mapping, not comprehension speed.
- **B1**: French subtitles only. Pause on any sentence you'd need to re-read twice. If that's happening more than once a minute, drop to a slower, more scripted show (learner-aimed YouTube channels beat native sitcoms at this stage).
- **B2+**: No subtitles for the first watch, French subtitles on a second pass only for scenes you didn't fully catch. This is where shadowing (repeating lines aloud immediately after hearing them) pays off — it trains rhythm and liaison, the two things subtitles can't teach.

### The 25% rule: cap passive watching

Set a hard ratio: no more than 25% of your weekly French time is pure watching. The other 75% goes to extraction, review, and output (speaking or writing). A learner doing 5 hours a week should watch for about 75 minutes and spend the rest processing it. This single rule fixes the most common failure mode — hours of exposure with nothing retained.

### Extraction: pull 10-15 items per episode, not 60

Don't pause every unknown word. Flag only words or phrases that are:
1. **Repeated** — appears 2+ times in the episode (signals it's common)
2. **Blocking** — you missed the meaning of a whole sentence because of it
3. **Useful outside this show** — "se débrouiller" (to manage/get by) travels; a one-off brand name doesn't

Write each one down with the full sentence it appeared in, not the word in isolation. "Il a fini par se débrouiller tout seul" teaches you the verb's reflexive form and its typical context — just "se débrouiller" on a list teaches you nothing you'll recall later.

### Review on a schedule, not on vibes

This is the step almost everyone skips. Extracted vocabulary that isn't reviewed decays within days — this is the forgetting curve Hermann Ebbinghaus described back in 1885, and it hasn't gotten kinder since. Spaced repetition systems counter it by resurfacing a word right before you'd forget it. A modern implementation like FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) adjusts the interval per card based on your actual recall history, so easy words stop wasting review slots and hard ones get seen more often.

### A realistic weekly routine (5 hours)

- **Mon/Wed/Fri (25 min each)**: Watch one 10-15 min YouTube segment or Netflix scene at your subtitle level. Extract 10-15 items.
- **Tue/Thu (15 min each)**: Flashcard review of everything extracted so far — not just this week's cards.
- **Sat (30 min)**: Re-watch one extraction-heavy scene with no subtitles; say the lines aloud with the audio.
- **Sun (20 min)**: Write 5 sentences using last week's flagged phrases in a new context.

### FAQ

**Can I learn French from Netflix alone?**
No. Netflix supplies input; it doesn't force retrieval. Without active recall of what you heard, most of it won't stick past a week.

**Should I watch dubbed or original French content?**
Original French productions (not English shows dubbed into French) — dubbing often uses unnatural rhythm and lip-sync-driven phrasing that doesn't match how French is actually spoken.

**How many new words per episode is too many?**
More than 20-25 flagged items per 20-minute episode usually means the content is above your level. Drop to something slower.

## Where Anki is the better choice

Anki has been the reference spaced repetition tool for almost two decades, and for good reason:

- **It works for anything.** Medicine, law, kanji, music theory — Anki doesn't care what is on the card.
- **It is endlessly customizable.** Card templates, fields, filtered decks and a large add-on ecosystem let you shape every detail.
- **It runs everywhere, including Android and desktop,** and its deck format is open and portable.
- **Huge shared deck library.** For popular languages you can often start from a community deck instead of an empty one.

If you enjoy building your own system, or you are learning a language Zenzu doesn't teach yet, Anki is hard to beat.

## Where Zenzu is the better choice

Most learners who drop Anki don't drop it because of the algorithm — they drop it because making good cards is slow. Zenzu removes that step:

- **Cards come from content you chose.** Paste a YouTube link, open a podcast episode or snap a photo of a page, then tap a word or select a phrase.
- **Context is kept automatically.** Each card carries the sentence it came from, and phrases get cloze deletion without manual formatting.
- **Pronunciation is built in.** Native text-to-speech plays for every word and sentence.
- **You learn from your own language.** Explanations and the AI coach work in English, Ukrainian, German, French, Spanish and Portuguese — not only through English.

## Can you use both?

Yes. Many learners keep Anki for non-language subjects or long-standing decks and use Zenzu for new vocabulary mined from what they watch and read. Because both use FSRS, review intervals feel similar across the two.
